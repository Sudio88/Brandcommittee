## Q1. Promo economics: net price, fees, contribution, break-even uplift

### Takeaway
On Thai marketplaces in 2026, the platform take before ads is already about 20 to 30% of the post-discount price. That is category commission plus a 3.21% payment fee, plus an optional free-shipping/voucher programme fee of about 5 to 9%, plus a per-order fee. Every discount layer therefore removes margin twice: once in the price, and again because some fees are computed on that price. Most fees scale down with the lower price, but some do not (fixed per-order fees, GWP cost, shipping subsidy). The break-even uplift rule is: required volume multiple = contribution per unit before ÷ contribution per unit after. Nielsen's widely cited figure is that most CPG promotions do not break even. Academic work shows a large part of the "bump" is pull-forward or stockpiling, not new demand.

**Evidence on whether promos pay back**
- Nielsen (2014) analysed 39 million promotional events, covering about USD 555bn of US retail sales, and found almost three-quarters of promotions do not break even. — [FoodNavigator-USA](https://www.foodnavigator-usa.com/Article/2014/10/09/Three-quarters-of-CPG-promotions-don-t-break-even-Nielsen)
- Nielsen's later figure is that 59% of trade promotions globally do not break even, rising to 71% or 72% in the US. — [FoodNavigator-USA 2016](https://www.foodnavigator-usa.com/Article/2016/04/04/Four-strategies-for-overcoming-diminishing-returns-on-trade-promotions/); [WARC](https://www.warc.com/newsandopinion/news/most-fmcg-promotions-lose-money/en-gb/34946). One 2026 blog argues the 59 to 72% figure lacks a traceable primary report. — [Koji blog](https://www.koji.so/blog/trade-promotion-effectiveness-research-2026)
- Bijmolt et al. (2005, meta-analysis) put the average short-term sales-promotion elasticity at −3.63. — cited via [Oxford Handbook of Pricing Management](https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/28115/chapter/212276183) search summary
- Blattberg, Briesch and Fox (1995), "How Promotions Work," *Marketing Science* 14(3) G122–G132, is the standard empirical-generalisations reference. Promotion effects on sales are "immediate and strong," which is why results-oriented managers favour them. — [Kellogg](https://www.kellogg.northwestern.edu/faculty/research/detail/1995/how-promotions-work/?p=1); [Neslin & van Heerde, *Promotion Dynamics* (2008)](https://www.nowpublishers.com/article/DownloadEBook/MKT-010)
- van Heerde, Leeflang and Wittink (2004, *Marketing Science*), "Decomposing the Sales Promotion Bump with Store Data," is the main paper for splitting lift into brand switching, category expansion and stockpiling or time-shifting. — [INFORMS](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/references/10.1287/mksc.1040.0061); [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/inm/ormksc/v23y2004i3p317-334.html)
- van Heerde, Gupta and Wittink (2003, JMR): about one-third of promotional unit lift comes from other brands, not the two-thirds implied by elasticity decomposition. This is from a secondary summary, so verify against the paper. — [Koji blog](https://www.koji.so/blog/trade-promotion-effectiveness-research-2026)
- Hendel and Nevo (QME, 2003): household data show stockpiling-driven post-promotion dips, but smaller than earlier estimates. In aggregate data the dip appears once feature and display are controlled for, because those effects mask it. — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1023/B:QMEC.0000004844.32036.5a)
- Bell, Chiang and Padmanabhan (1999, *Marketing Science*) generalise how promotion elasticity decomposes across brand choice, purchase incidence and stockpiling. — [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/inm/ormksc/v18y1999i4p504-526.html)
- Ailawadi and Neslin (1998, JMR), "The effect of promotion on consumption: buying more and consuming it faster": in variable-consumption categories, promotions can raise consumption as well as shift timing. — [INFORMS (Promotion Effect on Endogenous Consumption)](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/references/10.1287/mksc.1040.0110); [Springer](https://link.springer.com/doi/10.1007/BF00994076)
- Mela, Jedidi and Bowman: long-term promotion exposure reduced households' category purchase likelihood on later trips. When they do buy, they buy more, "lying in wait" for deals. This is reference-price and deal-conditioning evidence. — [Columbia (Jedidi pdf)](https://business.columbia.edu/sites/default/files-efs/pubfiles/3048/jedidi.pdf)
- Promotion-prone households show lower brand loyalty (older finding, cited secondarily). — [rpm.ndimdelhi.org](https://rpm.ndimdelhi.org/doi/pdf/10.1177/rpm.2014.12.1.62.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Net price waterfall (per unit, VAT-inclusive THB).** List price → minus seller discount = **deal price** → minus seller-funded shop voucher share → minus seller share of co-funded or platform vouchers (if any) = **seller-received item price (P_net)**. Then subtract:
  - commission % × P_net. Which base each platform applies the % to (pre- or post-platform-voucher) varies, so confirm per platform.
  - payment fee 3.21% × order total
  - programme fee (Shopee Xtra/Lazada Premium 5.35 to 8.56%; TikTok Growth Fee up to the THB 199 cap) × P_net
  - THB 1.07 per order
  - affiliate % (TikTok)
  - seller-paid shipping subsidy per order
  - GWP landed cost
  - COGS and pick-pack

  The result is **contribution per order (CPO)**.
- **Break-even uplift.** Required volume multiple = CPO_baseline ÷ CPO_promo. Required % uplift = (CPO_base ÷ CPO_promo − 1). Illustrative arithmetic only, not data: if baseline CPO is THB 60 and a stacked promo cuts it to THB 30, the promo must sell **2.0×** incremental baseline units, net of pull-forward and cannibalisation, just to stand still. With deal elasticity around −3.6 (Bijmolt), a 20% price cut "predicts" about +72% units. That is far short of the 2× needed, so deep stacked double-day deals on low-margin SKUs rarely pay on in-window economics alone.
- Fixed per-order costs (THB 1.07, shipping subsidy, GWP) bite hardest on low-AOV SKUs such as acne patches and single toothpaste. This argues for bundle and multipack thresholds over price cuts (see Q4).
- Because commission and programme fees are percentages, a discount does not cut fees in absolute terms by more than it cuts price. It is fixed costs plus COGS that make margin erosion steeper than the headline discount.
- A promo should be credited only with: (lift − pull-forward from own future sales − cannibalised own SKUs − sales that would have happened anyway via other channels or platforms).

### Gaps
- Beauty, oral-care and personal-care category-specific commission rates on each platform for Aug to Oct 2026 could not be confirmed. Search summaries gave only fashion and general rates.
- Whether Shopee and Lazada commission bases exclude platform-funded vouchers (they usually do) could not be verified from official pages.
- The Shopee "5% technical support fee" (Feb 2026) is unverified for Thailand.
- No Thailand or SEA-specific academic study of promo incrementality on marketplaces was found.

## Q2. Platform mechanics: campaign registration, minimum discounts, price checks, vouchers, free shipping, bundles, add-ons, GWP, paid packages

### Takeaway
All three platforms gate campaign and flash-sale entry with a "lowest price in last N days" rule and store-health thresholds. Free shipping and campaign vouchers are increasingly sold as paid, percentage-of-GMV programmes: Shopee Xtra/ร้านโค้ดคุ้ม at 5.35 to 8.56%, Lazada Premium Package at 8.56%, and TikTok Growth Fee at 6.96 to 8.03% capped at THB 199. Joining a double-day is therefore effectively a paid campaign package. Exact double-day minimum discounts for TH were not publicly findable and must be read from each campaign's Seller Centre brief.

### Inferences
- **The price-history rule is the binding constraint on double-day planning.** If Linee or Spiffy run a mid-month flash price, that price becomes the 14- or 30-day floor the next double-day must beat. Promo calendars should be planned backwards from 9.9, 10.10, 11.11 and 12.12, keeping the "reference low" above the intended campaign price. This is the most practical guard against an erosion spiral.
- Treat programme fees (Xtra, Premium, Growth Fee) as a standing cost of traffic access. In a double-day P&L they belong as part of the "campaign package" cost alongside the deeper discount.
- Prefer non-price mechanics where the platform allows them: bundle deals, add-on deals and GWP-as-a-tier. They do not reset the single-unit price floor in the same way, though how each platform's price check treats bundles is unverified.

### Gaps
- No official Shopee TH, Lazada TH or TikTok TH page was reachable. Exact 2025 to 2026 double-day minimum discount %, registration deadlines, "lowest price 30 days" rules for Shopee and Lazada TH, bundle-deal and add-on deal rules, GWP rules, and campaign package prices (for example Shopee Mall brand packages) were **not found**. These need to be pulled from Seller Centre campaign briefs.
- Co-funded voucher split ratios (platform vs seller) for TH double-days were not found.

## Q4. Bundle and price architecture; pricing premium brands without eroding them

### Takeaway
Good-better-best tiering protects premium tiers by giving price-sensitive buyers a stripped entry option rather than discounting the core. Blanket and recurring markdowns train buyers to wait. Evidence from BrandZ and the reference-price literature says heavy discounting damages equity, most of all for premium brands.

### Cited Findings
- GBB logic: a stripped "Good" option attracts new or price-sensitive customers, "Better" retains the core, and a feature-rich "Best" lifts spend. Firms crimp profit by discounting to attract price-sensitive buyers and by not giving high-end buyers reasons to spend more (Mohammed, *The Good-Better-Best Approach to Pricing*, HBR). — [Bookshare synopsis](https://bookshare.org/browse/book/2271405); [Wikipedia GBB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Good%E2%80%93better%E2%80%93best)
- Heavy discounting can be counter-productive and damage brand equity and the bottom line (BrandZ top 100 analysis, 2020). — [Marketing Week](https://www.marketingweek.com/premium-price-strategy-brand-z-2020/)
- Sweeping markdowns at arbitrary rates condition customers so they "will rarely pay full price." — [Retail TouchPoints](https://www.retailtouchpoints.com/topics/pricing/retail-pricing-is-a-science-not-an-art)
- The effect of discounting on equity depends on positioning (premium vs mid vs value) and on the price premium currently commanded. — [Branding Strategy Insider](https://brandingstrategyinsider.com/?p=24126)
- The simple price-premium measure tracks more complex brand-equity measures under certain conditions, so price premium vs category can serve as an equity KPI. — [De Gruyter, Review of Marketing Science](https://www.degruyterbrill.com/document/doi/10.1515/roms-2018-0009/pdf)
- Long-run promotion exposure makes households "lie in wait" for deals (Mela, Jedidi and Bowman). — [Columbia pdf](https://business.columbia.edu/sites/default/files-efs/pubfiles/3048/jedidi.pdf)
- Price-threshold effects matter in retail categories (*Marketing Science*, 2006), which supports deliberate price-ending choices. — [INFORMS](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/references/10.1287/mksc.1060.0207)

### Inferences (applied to the Moonshot portfolio; frameworks, not sourced data)
- **Multipacks fund the fee stack.** With a fixed THB 1.07 per-order fee, per-order shipping subsidy and GWP, plus a 3.21% payment fee, a 2-pack or 3-pack at a smaller per-unit discount usually beats a single-unit price cut on CPO. Examples are Spiffy patch packs and Linee toothpaste twin or triple packs. Set free-shipping or voucher minimum-spend thresholds just above the 2-pack price.
- **Loom by Linee (premium):** avoid headline % off. Use GWP (capped at a cost % of net price), a value set (bundle of hero plus mini) or a "Best" tier. That keeps the single-unit reference price intact for the 14 or 30-day price checks and for consumers.
- **Monchhichi (licensed):** royalty is typically a % of net sales, so discounts also shrink royalty. Model the minimum-guarantee position before deep discounts. This is an inference, so check the licence terms.
- Track **price premium vs category median** and the **% of units sold at full price** as equity guardrails.

### Gaps
- No Simon-Kucher, McKinsey or BCG study on SEA marketplace bundling or multipack economics was found.
- No Thai-specific evidence on price endings (for example THB 199/299 vs round numbers) was found.

## Q5. Designing a promo as an experiment

### Takeaway
Measure promos against a **concurrent** control (customer holdout, matched SKU/platform/geo control, or difference-in-differences). Do not use last-period comparisons, which overstate lift. Net out pre-period differences, measure cannibalisation at category or SKU level, and keep the read window open 60 to 90 days so pull-forward and repeat behaviour show up.

### Cited Findings
- Use a concurrent holdout, not before-vs-after. Withhold the promotion from 10 to 20% of the eligible audience. Lift = exposed minus holdout, net of pre-period baseline differences. Period-over-period comparisons "systematically overstate promotional value." — [Talon.One](https://www.talon.one/blog/measure-promotion-roi); [Enactor](https://insights.enactor.co/wp-content/uploads/devdoc/docs/retail-knowledge/loyalty-and-promotions/promotion-performance-measurement-incrementality/)
- Use geo or matched-market holdouts and difference-in-differences where user-level holdouts are impractical. Synthetic controls avoid holdout cost but rest on modelling assumptions. — [Enactor](https://insights.enactor.co/wp-content/uploads/devdoc/docs/retail-knowledge/loyalty-and-promotions/promotion-performance-measurement-incrementality/); [Epsilon](https://www.epsilon.com/emea/insights/blog/how-to-measure-incrementality-retail-media)
- Illustrative example: a 20% uplift that cannibalises 18% of full-price purchases in the same category delivers almost no net value. — [Uniqodo](https://www.uniqodo.com/glossary/what-is-incrementality)
- Stockpiling and pull-forward: when the post-promo trough offsets the uplift, total-period incrementality can be near zero. — [Racklify](https://racklify.com/encyclopedia/what-incremental-sales-means-and-how-ecommerce-teams-measure-it/)
- For discounts, track orders, AOV, returns and repeat purchase for 60 to 90 days. Control for price changes, external events and overlapping campaigns. — [Talon.One](https://www.talon.one/blog/measure-promotion-roi)
- Distributed-lag methods for pre- and post-promotion dips: van Heerde, Leeflang and Wittink (2000), "The Estimation of Pre- and Postpromotion Dips with Store-Level Scanner Data." — [arXiv citing paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.25664)

### Inferences (proposed Moonshot promo-test protocol)
1. **Pre-registration card** before any promo ships. It should state:
   - hypothesis
   - mechanic
   - expected CPO_promo vs CPO_base
   - required break-even multiple
   - stated loss cap in THB
   - read date
   - kill criteria
2. **Baseline:** trailing 28-day median daily units and GMV per SKU per platform, excluding campaign days, with a same-weekday adjustment.
3. **Control:** use whichever fits.
   - A platform holdout: run the promo on Shopee only and use TikTok or Lazada as the DiD control. The caveat is cross-platform switching.
   - A SKU holdout: a matched non-promoted SKU within the brand.
   - Customer-level: send a shop voucher to 80 to 90% of followers or CRM buyers and hold out 10 to 20%.
4. **Windows:** pre-window (−14 days) to detect pre-promo dips from announced campaigns, promo window, post-window (+14 to +30 days) for pull-forward, and a 60 to 90 day repeat read.
5. **Decomposition:** report incremental units = promo-window lift − post-window deficit − cannibalised sister-SKU units. Split buyers into new-to-brand vs repeat using platform buyer IDs. Report the repeat rate of promo-acquired new buyers vs organically acquired ones.
6. **Verdict:** incremental contribution = incremental units × CPO_promo − (baseline units that took the discount × (CPO_base − CPO_promo)) − fixed campaign package cost. The promo is a "pass" only if this is above zero, or if it is below the stated loss cap and the new-buyer LTV case is explicit.

### Gaps
- No marketplace-specific (Shopee, Lazada, TikTok) tooling for randomised customer holdouts was confirmed. Whether shop-voucher targeting to follower segments supports a clean holdout is unverified.
- No published Thai brand case of a measured promo holdout was found.
