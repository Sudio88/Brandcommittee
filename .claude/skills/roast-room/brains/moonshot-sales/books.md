## Promotions and pull-forward

| Title | Author / publisher | Why it matters | Grade | Id |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| "How Promotions Work", *Marketing Science* 14(3) (1995) | Blattberg, Briesch & Fox | Empirical generalisations: promo effects are immediate and strong, which is why teams over-use them. | PRIMARY | S049 |
| "Decomposing the Sales Promotion Bump with Store Data", *Marketing Science* (2004) | van Heerde, Leeflang & Wittink | Split lift into switching, category expansion and stockpiling; the basis for "incremental" in our readouts. | PRIMARY | S038 |
| Meta-analysis of price and promotion elasticity (2005) | Bijmolt et al. | Short-term promo elasticity ~−3.63; a US CPG prior, never a Moonshot forecast. | PRIMARY | S037 |
| Long-term promotion exposure and deal-waiting | Mela, Jedidi & Bowman | Frequent deals train buyers to wait; supports "less is more" promos. | PRIMARY | S050 |
| Nielsen trade promotion studies (2014, 2016) | via FoodNavigator-USA, WARC | ~¾ of US CPG promos do not break even; 59% globally (primary untraced). | SECONDARY | S035, S036 |
| Pull-forward and hangover | Recast; Gladly; arXiv 2508.00208 | Pre- and post-periods can swamp the sale; promo-acquired customers spend less afterwards. | PRACTITIONER / PRIMARY | S012, S029, S013 |
| Promotion ROI with concurrent holdouts | Talon.One | Holdout design for every package and double-day. | PRACTITIONER | S039 |
