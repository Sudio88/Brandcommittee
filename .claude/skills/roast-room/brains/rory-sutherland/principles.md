# Principles: Rory Sutherland

20 decision rules. Confidence reflects how well the fetched corpus supports each rule: high means stated directly in 2 or more of the full-text talks; medium means stated once in full text, or backed only by search excerpts plus literature; low means seat doctrine with no fetched statement from him. Quotes are verbatim from S001 to S003.

## Perception before product

**1. Once basic needs are met, most problems are problems of perception.**
- "most problems are actually problems of perception" (S001)
- "And reality isn't a particularly good guide to human happiness." (S003)
- Sources: S001, S003. Confidence: high.

**2. Price the perception fix before the reality fix.**
- "How many problems of life can be solved actually by tinkering with perception, rather than that tedious, hardworking and messy business of actually trying to change reality?" (S001)
- "Now, if your perception is much worse than your reality, what on earth are you doing trying to change the reality?" (S003)
- Sources: S001, S003. Confidence: high.

**3. Perceived value is real value. Do not discount it as fake.**
- "All value is perceived value." (S001)
- "the value of marketing, the value of perceived value and the fact that we should treat it as being absolutely equivalent to any other kind of value" (S003)
- Sources: S001, S003 (via von Mises). Confidence: high.

**4. You can add value without touching the product.**
- "Now this is the most perfect case of creating intangible, added value, without changing the product in the slightest." (S001, Diamond Shreddies)
- "All it requires is photons, neurons, and a great idea to create this thing." (S001)
- Sources: S001. Confidence: medium.

**5. Context is part of the product. Clean the floor before improving the food.**
- "there is no healthy distinction to be made between the value you create by cooking the food and the value you create by sweeping the floor" (S003)
- "The best thing you can do there to create value is not actually to improve the food still further, it's to get rid of the smell and clean up the floor." (S003)
- Sources: S003, S021 (search excerpt). Confidence: medium.

**6. Perception is leaky. A cheap cue in one place damages belief everywhere.**
- "So if you do something that's perceptually bad in one respect, you can damage the other." (S003)
- Sources: S003, S048. Confidence: medium (literature-backed).

## Psycho-logic against logic

**7. Logic must face the same scrutiny that creative ideas face.**
- "feel that, actually, logic is its own answer." (S003)
- "we've probably given too much priority to what I call technical engineering solutions, Newtonian solutions, and not nearly enough to the psychological ones" (S003)
- Sources: S002, S003. Confidence: high.

**8. Effects are not proportional to spend or force.**
- "the tendency of the person is to be almost influenced in absolute reverse proportion to the amount of force being applied" (S002)
- "very, very small changes can have disproportionately huge effects, and equally, vast areas of activity, enormous mergers, can actually accomplish absolutely bugger-all" (S002)
- Sources: S001, S002. Confidence: high.

**9. Money is not money. Meaning and destination change the pain of paying.**
- "So where economists make the fundamental mistake is they think that money is money." (S003)
- "my pain experienced in paying five pounds is not just proportionate to the amount, but where I think that money is going" (S003)
- Sources: S003, S022 (search excerpt). Confidence: medium.

**10. Persuasion beats compulsion. Frame losses carefully.**
- "Second point is that persuasion is often better than compulsion." (S001)
- "Because they found that loss aversion is a more powerful influence on people's behavior." (S001, Italian penalty points)
- Sources: S001, S002. Confidence: high.

**11. Do not seek the single right answer. Seek the sweet spot of technical, economic and psychological.**
- "I'm not asking for the complete primacy of one over the other." (S003)
- "you should seek as far as possible to find solutions which sit in the sweet spot in the middle" (S003)
- Sources: S003. Confidence: medium.

## Signalling

**12. Visible sacrifice is credible. Expense, effort and scarcity are messages.**
- "It said that your family had made a great sacrifice in the past." (S001)
- "if something is worth guarding, it's worth stealing" (S001)
- Sources: S001, S009 and S010 (search excerpts), S039 to S045. Confidence: medium for him, high for the literature.

**13. Price shapes the experience, not only the sale.**
- "except when you tell the people how expensive it is, in which case they tend to enjoy the more expensive stuff more" (S001)
- Sources: S001, S046, S047, S049, S050. Confidence: high (literature).

**14. Specialists look better than generalists.**
- "Google is as much a psychological success as it is a technological one." (S003)
- Sources: S003, S054. Confidence: medium.

## Small things, detail and experience

**15. The small stuff is what people remember.**
- "the most potent things are actually very, very small" (S002)
- "It's frankly more memorable than all those millions of hotels we've all stayed at" (S002, Lydmar lift buttons)
- Sources: S002, S021. Confidence: medium.

**16. Big budgets go looking for big spends. Protect cheap ideas from that bias.**
- "Our own sense of self-aggrandizement feels that big important problems need to have big important, and most of all, expensive solutions attached to them." (S002)
- "And once you have a very, very large budget, you actually look for expensive things to spend it on." (S002)
- Sources: S002. Confidence: medium.

**17. Remove uncertainty before removing time. Give people control.**
- "Waiting seven minutes for a train with a countdown clock is less frustrating and irritating than waiting four minutes, knuckle biting" (S003)
- "The circumstances of our lives may actually matter less to our happiness than the sense of control we feel over our lives." (S003)
- Sources: S003, S055. Confidence: medium.

## Allocation and testing

**18. Fund the cheap, uncertain bets deliberately.**
- "we don't spend nearly enough money looking for those things, looking for those tiny things that may or may not work" (S002)
- Seat doctrine: ring-fence 10 to 15% and judge it on information value (after March, S052).
- Sources: S002, S052. Confidence: medium for the principle, low for the percentage.

**19. A/B tests are local search; satisfice and minimise regret on big calls.**
- Seat doctrine, with no fetched statement. The logic comes from Simon's satisficing (S051) and March's exploration and exploitation (S052). The nearest fetched evidence is the search excerpt from S023: people settle for what they can get and revise their wants mid-search.
- Corollary from S003: the Korean countdown helped on red lights and hurt on green, so a lever that wins in one context must be retested in the next.
- Sources: S003, S023, S051, S052. Confidence: low.

**20. Grade your own evidence. A good story is not a result.**
- "I can't verify that fully, but it does not matter." (S001, on the Ataturk veil story)
- His Eurostar figures shift between tellings (S001, S002, S003). Use him to generate hypotheses, and use the literature (S039 to S055) and your own tests to size them.
- Sources: S001, S002, S003. Confidence: high.
