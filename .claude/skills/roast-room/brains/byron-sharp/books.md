# Books: Byron Sharp and Jenni Romaniuk

Built only from public descriptions, the papers behind the books, and how the authors teach them. No book text is reproduced. Details not confirmed in this build are marked.

## How Brands Grow: What Marketers Don't Know (Byron Sharp, 2010, Oxford University Press)

- **Core thesis:** Brands grow mainly by increasing penetration. Loyalty follows brand size (double jeopardy). Growth comes from building physical and mental availability and reaching all category buyers, with distinctive branding.
- **Key frameworks:** penetration-led growth, double jeopardy, the buyer base and light buyers, no brand segmentation, physical and mental availability, reach all category buyers. See frameworks.md #1 to #5, #7, #11.
- **Taught in:** S001 (answering critics), S009 (legacy interview: the idea most adopted is that growth needs more customers), S031 (misreadings), S033 (loyalty exists).
- **Evidence base:** Ehrenberg's work on double jeopardy and buyer behaviour (S014, S035) and Institute replications (S015, S047).

## How Brands Grow Part 2 (Jenni Romaniuk and Byron Sharp, 2015/2016, Oxford University Press)

- **Core thesis:** Restates the laws and extends the practical side, notably Category Entry Points as the building blocks of mental availability.
- **Key frameworks:** CEPs, mental availability measurement, distinctive assets. See frameworks.md #8.
- **Taught in:** S016, S017 (Romaniuk on CEPs). Emerging-market evidence of the same family: S018.
- **Not verified in this build:** exact chapter coverage of emerging markets, services and durables, and the publication year (sources give 2015 for Australia and 2016 elsewhere).

## Building Distinctive Brand Assets (Jenni Romaniuk, 2018, Oxford University Press)

- **Core thesis:** Brands need assets (colours, shapes, characters, logos, sounds) that category buyers link to the brand and only the brand. Judge them on fame and uniqueness.
- **Key frameworks:** the Distinctive Asset grid with four quadrants (Use or Lose, Investment Potential, Avoid Solo Use, Ignore or Test), as reproduced in later research (S025). See frameworks.md #10.
- **Later evidence:** 2026 benchmarks of average fame and uniqueness (S026).

## Better Brand Health (Jenni Romaniuk, 2022, Oxford University Press)

- **Core thesis:** Replace awareness and attitude tracking with metrics built on CEPs that link to buying behaviour.
- **Key frameworks:** Mental Market Share, Mental Penetration, Network Size, Share of Mind (S029, S030). See frameworks.md #9.
- **Not verified in this build:** exact release date; one interview dates it 2023, the publisher listing and most commentary point to 2022.
