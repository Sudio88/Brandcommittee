# Sources: Byron Sharp and the Ehrenberg-Bass Institute

47 sources: peer-reviewed papers (abstracts and catalog records), Ehrenberg-Bass Institute posts, Sharp's own blog and LinkedIn articles, Jenni Romaniuk's blog, trade coverage (Marketing Week, WARC, Campaign, Mi3), and the documented counterpoints (Mark Ritson, Les Binet and Peter Field, Dunnhumby). No book text is reproduced; the four books are covered through public descriptions and the papers behind them.

**Method note (read before quoting).** Built 2026-10-16. Direct page fetching was blocked in this session (DNS and egress policy refused marketingscience.info, byronsharp.wordpress.com, wikipedia and others), so every source was read through web-search page extracts of the URL listed, not a full-page read. Quotes in this brain are limited to wording that appeared verbatim in those extracts. Where an extract gave only a paraphrase, the brain paraphrases and cites, and does not use quote marks. Rows marked "title-level" were seen only as a title and catalog line: cite them for existence and headline claim only. Before using any quote in public, check it against the original page. A search budget cap ended research before planned searches on Romaniuk's differentiation work, the Dawes promotion papers, and How Brands Grow Part 2's emerging-market chapters; those gaps are named in the files where they matter.

**Evidence grades used across this brain.**
- **A (law-like):** replicated across many categories, countries and decades in peer-reviewed work.
- **B (published, narrower):** peer-reviewed, but one category, one country, one program, or a short run of replications.
- **C (Institute framework):** a measurement or planning framework built on A or B findings, with practitioner use but few published causal tests.
- **D (argument):** Sharp's or Romaniuk's reasoning in talks, posts and interviews; informed, not tested as stated.
- **Inference:** this seat's application to Moonshot's channels. Never present it as an Ehrenberg-Bass finding.
