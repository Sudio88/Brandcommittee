# Frameworks: Jay Conrad Levinson

14 frameworks, grouped by the job they do (substitute, grow, combine, persist, partner, measure, guard). "Sources" counts distinct sources in the corpus that carry the framework or a clear synonym. Book tags show where the framework sits in his series. Evidence grade for every framework: conceptual (practitioner doctrine, no controlled tests).

Levinson's guerrilla is tactical and budgetary. Do not import Ries and Trout's positional guerrilla (defend a small segment, never act like the leader, be ready to retreat) into these frameworks; that is a separate 1986 doctrine (S022, S023).

---

## Substitute

### 1. Substitution test: time, energy, imagination, information for money *(Guerrilla Marketing, 1984 onward)*
- **What it is:** Before spending, ask what unconventional, cheaper input could buy the same result. The small firm's edge is effort and wit, not budget.
- **Parts:** Time (founder and team hours, follow-up, community). Energy (persistence, events, live selling). Imagination (a sharper idea, a surprising angle, a partner). Information (knowing the customer, the niche and the numbers better than the big player). Later books add information explicitly, as in "time, energy, imagination, and information" (S041).
- **When to use:** Any budget request, any argument that the answer is more ad spend, any launch plan with a big paid line.
- **How to score:** List each paid line. For each, write the non-money substitute and its cost in hours. If a credible substitute exists and the team has the hours, the paid line must justify itself on profit, not habit.
- **Quote:** "the theory of guerrilla marketing is that your primary investments should be time, energy and imagination" (S008); the books' promise is "substituting time, energy, and imagination for money" (S040).
- **Sources:** 6 (S008, S025, S032, S038, S040, S041)

### 2. Unconventional means, small targets *(Guerrilla Marketing)*
- **What it is:** Aim at individuals and small groups rather than masses, because the small firm cannot win the mass-media auction.
- **Parts:** Narrow the audience; personalise the message; use direct, human contact.
- **When to use:** Choosing between broad awareness and tight, owned audiences (followers, LINE OA, past buyers).
- **How to score:** The smaller and more reachable the target group, the higher the score, provided it can carry profit.
- **Quote:** "Guerrilla marketing aims its message at individuals or, if it must be a group, the smaller the group, the better." (S011)
- **Sources:** 3 (S011, S013, S014)

## Grow

### 3. Geometric growth *(Guerrilla Marketing, 4th ed.)*
- **What it is:** Grow by multiplying the value of customers you already have, not only by adding new ones.
- **Parts:** (1) Larger transactions per sale. (2) More transactions per customer per cycle. (3) Referrals from each customer. (4) Ordinary new-customer growth, last (S038). Fusion partners widen the offer so these levers multiply (S007).
- **When to use:** Any growth plan, any plan that leans only on acquisition, and before raising CAC.
- **How to score:** Count how many of the three geometric levers the option pulls and estimate the profit lift of each. An option that pulls none is linear growth and must clear a higher bar.
- **Quote:** fusion lets "by partnering with others, a company can expand its product or service offerings" (S007)
- **Sources:** 4 (S001, S007, S038, S046)

### 4. The subsequent sale *(Secret 7, "subsequent")*
- **What it is:** Profit lives after the first order, in repeat and referral business.
- **Parts:** First order as acquisition; follow-up; repeat; referral; customer service as the marketing.
- **When to use:** Pricing a first-order offer, judging a promo that buys one-time buyers, designing post-purchase flows.
- **How to score:** Estimate repeat rate and referral rate of the buyers this tactic attracts. A tactic that wins first orders from deal hunters who never return scores low even at good GMV.
- **Quote:** "The real profits come after you've made the sale, in the form of repeat and referral business." (S001)
- **Sources:** 6 (S001, S002, S003, S025, S033, S047)

### 5. Follow-up calendar *(articles and 4th ed.)*
- **What it is:** A fixed, dated contact schedule after every sale.
- **Parts:** "When a guerrilla makes a sale, the customer receives a follow-up thank-you note within 48 hours." Then "The guerrilla sends another note or perhaps makes a phone call 30 days after the sale." Then "After six months, the customer hears from the guerrilla again, this time with the preview announcement of an upcoming sale." Then "Nine months after the sale, the guerrilla sends a note asking the customer for the names of three people who might benefit" (all S002).
- **When to use:** Any channel where we can reach the buyer again (D2C, LINE OA, shop followers, live-room regulars).
- **How to score:** Does the plan name each touch, its date and its owner? Missing touches are lost profit.
- **Quote:** "For this reason, guerrilla marketing preaches fervent follow-up" (S003)
- **Sources:** 5 (S002, S003, S006, S025, S030)

## Combine

### 6. Weapons arsenal (100 to 200 weapons) *(Guerrilla Marketing Attack, 1989; later editions)*
- **What it is:** A long menu of low-cost tactics grouped by media type, from which the firm picks, tests and keeps a mix.
- **Parts:** Attack (1989) has a chapter "One Hundred Weapons for the Attack" (S042). The 4th edition groups weapons into minimedia, maximedia, e-media, info-media, human-media and nonmedia (S043). Reprinted lists run to 200 (S044, S045). His spa article draws 20 keys from "a full 120 described in my book" (S006).
- **When to use:** Building a quarter plan; when a team leans on one channel.
- **How to score:** Count distinct weapons in the plan, the share that are free or near free, and how many reinforce each other.
- **Quote:** "Guerrilla marketing requires you to comprehend every facet of marketing, experiment with many of them" (S012)
- **Sources:** 7 (S006, S012, S037, S042, S043, S044, S045)

### 7. Marketing combinations *(Secret 6, "assortment")*
- **What it is:** No single weapon carries the business; weapons multiply each other.
- **Parts:** Pick a set that fits the market and budget, since you "can't launch and implement every single marketing weapon there is" (S037); run them together; let each raise the others.
- **When to use:** When someone proposes a single big bet (one KOL, one ad burst, one stunt).
- **How to score:** For the proposed tactic, name the two or three other weapons it feeds and that feed it. Standalone tactics score low.
- **Quote:** "Guerrillas know that marketing combinations work." and "they'll all work, and they'll each help the others work." (S004)
- **Sources:** 5 (S004, S033, S037, S044, S049)

## Persist

### 8. Commitment, consistency, patience *(Secrets 1, 3, 5)*
- **What it is:** Choose a plan, keep it consistent, and wait long enough for repetition to build familiarity and confidence.
- **Parts:** Commitment to the program; consistency of identity and message; patience; confidence in the buyer's mind as the result (S033, S034). The 4th edition's exposure rule: roughly 27 exposures to reach a prospect's mind nine times before purchase (S001, rhetorical rule, not measured).
- **When to use:** Every time a team wants to change a working campaign because it is bored, or kill a new one before it has run.
- **How to score:** Planned duration versus the time needed to accumulate exposures; number of creative or identity changes per quarter (fewer is better unless results say otherwise).
- **Quote:** "The difference between many a success and failure is a Guerrilla Marketing Plan and a commitment to continue to do what's working." (S025)
- **Sources:** 7 (S001, S010, S025, S033, S034, S035, S047)

### 9. Marketing as process, augmented *(Secrets 15 and 16; "process" maxim)*
- **What it is:** Marketing is never finished; a working program is improved, not abandoned.
- **Parts:** Start, run, measure, augment. "After you have full-fledged marketing programs, work to AUGMENT it rather than rest on your laurels." (S034)
- **When to use:** Post-launch reviews; when a channel plateaus.
- **How to score:** Does the plan add to what works, or replace it? Replacement needs evidence.
- **Quote:** "It has a beginning, a middle, but never an end, for it is a process." (S010)
- **Sources:** 4 (S009, S010, S014, S034)

## Partner

### 10. Fusion marketing and power partners *(Secret 12, "dependent"; Guerrilla Marketing and Joint Ventures)*
- **What it is:** Team up with non-competing businesses that share your customers to share reach, costs and offers.
- **Parts:** Power partners with like customers; cross-links and shared content; shared displays and joint events; joint ventures.
- **When to use:** Any time we would otherwise pay for reach into an audience someone else already owns.
- **How to score:** Audience overlap, brand fit, cost to us, profit share, and who owns the customer afterwards.
- **Quote:** "Power partners are another example of fusion marketing: two companies working with like customers and like prospects in synergistic fashion." (S007); a later book calls partnering "the most powerful weapon in the guerrilla marketing arsenal" (S039).
- **Sources:** 6 (S007, S025, S031, S033, S036, S039)

## Measure

### 11. Profit as the only scoreboard *(Secret 10, "measurement")*
- **What it is:** Measure each weapon by profit, not sales, responses, traffic or applause.
- **Parts:** Track each weapon; cut losers; reinvest in winners; read creativity by profit too: "creativity should be measured solely by how well it contributes to your overall profitability" (S027).
- **When to use:** Every review, every KOL or live-commerce settlement, every promo calendar.
- **How to score:** Contribution margin per weapon after discounts, platform fees, commissions, returns and samples.
- **Quote:** "Profits are the only numbers that tell you the truth" (S005)
- **Sources:** 6 (S005, S025, S027, S046, S048, S050)

### 12. Relationship count *(house doctrine)*
- **What it is:** Alongside profit, count the relationships built, because they are the source of the subsequent sale.
- **Parts:** Buyers who reorder, follow, join the community, refer. Traditional marketers count money at the month's end; guerrillas count new relationships (paraphrase of S050). "Guerrillas place emphasis on retaining existing customers instead of solely focusing on acquiring new ones" (S025).
- **When to use:** Comparing a GMV spike with a smaller campaign that builds owned audience.
- **How to score:** Net new reachable customers (owned channels) per baht spent.
- **Quote:** "Most businesses lose customers by ignoring them to death." (S002)
- **Sources:** 4 (S002, S025, S047, S050)

## Guard

### 13. Amazement with a budget fence *(Secret 9, "amazement", plus house rule)*
- **What it is:** Put an element of surprise into marketing (S033), but never at the expense of continuity.
- **Parts:** Amazement lives in details buyers take for granted (packaging, inserts, service gestures) as much as in stunts. Stunts come from the exploration budget, never the continuity budget. Every stunt must name its brand clearly, be legal, and pass a check on whether it could be mistaken for a threat, a hoax or a lie.
- **When to use:** Any stunt, flash mob, street placement, or viral bet.
- **How to score:** Attribution (will people know it is us?), safety and legality, profit path (how does attention become repeat buyers?), and budget source.
- **Quote:** none verbatim beyond the secret's name; see the Boston 2007 caution in stories.md (S018, S019, S020, S021).
- **Sources:** 6 (S018, S019, S020, S021, S033, S049)

### 14. Consent and content *(Secrets 14 and 15)*
- **What it is:** Market to people who agreed to hear from you, and sell the substance of the offer, not its style.
- **Parts:** Earn permission first, then market to those who gave it; let the offer's real content do the work (S049, S033).
- **When to use:** CRM, broadcast and retargeting plans; any claim-heavy creative.
- **How to score:** Share of outreach going to opted-in audiences; whether every claim is true and substantiated.
- **Quote:** "Guerrillas practice fervent followup." (S025), always inside consent.
- **Sources:** 3 (S025, S033, S049)
