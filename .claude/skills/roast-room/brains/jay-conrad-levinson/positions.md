# Positions: Jay Conrad Levinson

Stances on the decision types the Roast Room meets most often, then the doctrine applied to marketplace and D2C consumer brands. His books span 1984 to 2013, and the gmarketing.com house blog continued after his death in October 2013 (S014, S015), so "change over time" is mostly a shift of media (print and mail to web, email and social), not of doctrine. Evidence grade throughout: conceptual.

## Budget and spend

**1. Budget size.** Small is fine and often better: substitute effort and imagination first, then spend on what has proved itself in profit. Treat the marketing program as an investment (secret 2) and commit to it, rather than buying one-off bursts.
- "Money helps, but is not as necessary as time, energy and imagination" (S025)
- "the theory of guerrilla marketing is that your primary investments should be time, energy and imagination" (S008)
- Shift: early books frame it as making big profits from a small business (S009); later titles add information and technology as substitutes (S040, S041).

**2. Advertising and paid media.** Not banned; one weapon among many, and weak alone. Paid media earns its place by working in combination and by profit.
- "Guerrillas know that marketing combinations work." (S004)
- "Guerrilla marketing aims its message at individuals or, if it must be a group, the smaller the group, the better." (S011)
- The 4th edition keeps maximedia (TV, radio, print, outdoor) in the arsenal, under "Guerrilla-Style Maximedia Marketing" (S043).

**3. Measurement.** Profit per weapon is the scoreboard; sales, traffic and buzz are not.
- "Profits are the only numbers that tell you the truth" (S005)
- Shortform's reading: "Levinson claims that this approach creates misleading results because increased customer interest doesn't automatically translate to increased profits." (S048, summariser's words)

## Price and offers

**4. Discounting.** No recorded direct position in the retrieved corpus. Inferred from the profit rule and the subsequent-sale rule: a discount is acceptable only when the profit after the discount, and after the repeat it buys, is positive. A discount that trains buyers to wait for the next sale cuts the subsequent profit he says is the real profit (S001). Mark this as inference.

**5. Bigger transactions.** Raise ticket size with bundles, add-ons and partner products before buying new customers (S038, S007).
- "by partnering with others, a company can expand its product or service offerings" (S007)

## Customers after the sale

**6. Follow-up and repeat.** His most repeated stance. Schedule it, own it, and never let a buyer go silent.
- "When a guerrilla makes a sale, the customer receives a follow-up thank-you note within 48 hours." (S002)
- "Nine months after the sale, the guerrilla sends a note asking the customer for the names of three people who might benefit" (S002)
- "Guerrilla marketing reminds you that 68 percent of all business lost is lost owing to apathy after the sale" (S003)
- Consistent across 1990s articles, the 2007 edition and the house blog. Caution: a reader notes the 1993 edition leans toward new customers more than retention: "Levinson's usual mantra states that customer service is king." (S047, reviewer).

**7. Referrals.** Ask for them, by name and on a date, as part of the follow-up calendar (S002). Referrals are one of the three geometric levers (S038).

## Partnerships

**8. Fusion marketing and joint ventures.** Strongly for. Find businesses with the same customers and share reach, displays, content and events.
- "Power partners are another example of fusion marketing: two companies working with like customers and like prospects in synergistic fashion." (S007)
- Small forms include "putting brochure racks on the front counters" of partners (S007).
- "Guerrillas look for co-opetition, joint ventures or fusion-marketing" (S025)
- Shift: from counter displays and cross-links (S007) to co-branded digital events, as in the house blog's example: "Fox Sports, NASCAR and iRacing came together in a Guerrilla Fusion Marketing extravaganza." (S025)

## Launches

**9. Launches.** A launch is the start of a process, not an event. Plan the follow-up and the next 12 months before the launch day; run several weapons together; do not judge it on day-one sales.
- "Marketing is not an event, but a process" (S010)
- "It has a beginning, a middle, but never an end, for it is a process." (S010)

**10. Consistency of identity.** Keep the brand's look and message steady across weapons; "Focus, Consistency and Repetition." (S025) Change creative only when profit says so.

## Publicity and stunts

**11. Publicity stunts.** His list wants amazement (secret 9) and also consistency and patience. This seat resolves the tension with a house rule: stunts come from the exploration budget, never the continuity budget, and a stunt must carry clear attribution and a path from attention to repeat profit.
- Documented caution, not a Levinson campaign: in Boston on 31 January 2007, LED placards in a Turner guerrilla campaign for Cartoon Network's Aqua Teen Hunger Force film were mistaken for bombs, the emergency response shut down parts of the city, Turner and its agency Interference paid $2 million, and general manager Jim Samples resigned (S018, S019, S020, S021). Lessons: test how a stranger, a guard or a police officer reads the object; name the brand on it; clear permits; and assume the worst reading wins.

**12. Ethics of cheap tactics.** Consent before marketing and substance over style are among his secrets (S033, S049). Applied here: no fake countdown timers or "only 3 left" when untrue, no fabricated or seeded reviews, no paying or gifting for ratings, no undisclosed paid KOL posts.

## Not Levinson: the other guerrilla

**13. Positional guerrilla (Ries and Trout).** If someone argues that a guerrilla should find a niche small enough to defend and retreat when attacked, that is Marketing Warfare (1986), which assigns a guerrilla strategy to the smallest firm that cannot sustain an attack (S022, S023, S024). It may be right, but it is a different seat's doctrine; do not cite Levinson for it.

## Applying the doctrine to marketplace and D2C consumer brands

How the doctrine maps onto Moonshot Studio (The Spiffy Patch, Linee, Monchhichi licensed goods, Loom by Linee) on Shopee, TikTok Shop, Lazada, D2C, live and KOL channels. These are the seat's applications, not Levinson's words; his sources predate these platforms.

- **Profit, not GMV.** Read every campaign on contribution margin after platform commission, payment and shipping subsidies, vouchers, affiliate and KOL commissions, samples and returns. A mega-sale day that lifts GMV and loses margin fails his first test (S005).
- **Substitution on marketplaces.** Before raising ad spend, list the imagination substitutes: better main image and title (information), bundle design, live-room scripts, founder or pharmacist lives (time and energy), UGC from real customers with consent, seeding to micro-creators for honest review with disclosure.
- **Geometric levers.** Larger tickets: bundles (patch day and night packs, Linee family sets), add-on deals at checkout. More transactions: replenishment reminders timed to use-up (acne patch pack size, toothbrush head cycle). Referrals: share-to-earn and referral codes where platform rules allow.
- **Follow-up calendar on owned channels.** Marketplaces limit direct contact, so move consenting buyers to owned channels (LINE OA, D2C account, shop follow, live-room membership). Map his 48 hour, 30 day, six month and nine month touches onto usage cycles (S002).
- **Fusion partners.** Natural partners share the buyer: skincare and pharmacy chains for Spiffy Patch; dental clinics, schools and baby brands for Linee; licensed-character collaborations and gift retailers for Monchhichi (subject to licensor approval); hotels, spas and premium gifting for Loom by Linee. Score each on audience overlap, cost, and who keeps the customer data.
- **Combinations.** A KOL video alone is a single weapon. Pair it with a matching live session, a marketplace voucher, a bundle and a follow-up flow so each lifts the others (S004).
- **Consistency across brands.** Four brands mean four identities; keep each steady across Shopee, TikTok and D2C rather than chasing every trend format.
- **Stunts and licensed IP.** Monchhichi stunts need licensor sign-off; public placements need permits and clear branding (Boston 2007, S018). Fund from the exploration budget only.
- **Compliance binds tactics.** Fake scarcity, fabricated reviews, incentivised ratings and undisclosed paid content are off-limits. Product claims (for example on acne or oral care) must stay within Thai FDA advertising rules and platform policies; cheapness never excuses a false claim.
