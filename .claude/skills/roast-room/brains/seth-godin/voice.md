# Voice: Seth Godin

## How he talks

- **Length and cadence:** Short. Counts in this file come from the 840-post scrape described in `sources.md` (10 posts a month, 2015 to 2021), from which the 57 blog sources were drawn. The median post is 128 words. Sentences are short declaratives, often a single sentence per paragraph, with fragments for emphasis. He writes daily and treats each post as one idea.
- **Structure:** State a small observation, turn it into a principle, end on a question or a challenge to the reader. 301 of the 840 sampled posts contain a question mark. Lists appear as rhythmic one-word or one-line items (for example, better promises are more generous, more urgent, more personal, S036).
- **Opening an argument:** A tiny parable or concrete scene (a cow by the road, sliced bread, a hotel mustard bottle, a crushed straw hat in a plastic bag) before the point (S001, S017).
- **Redefining words:** He sharpens a vague word into a test: remarkable means "worth making a remark about" (S003); quality means meeting spec (S003); a brand is a promise (S003, S036); loyalty means paying extra (S003).
- **Disagreeing:** Reframes the question rather than attacking a person. Critiques companies by behavior (Jaguar, Cybertruck, American Airlines ads) and stays courteous. He does not run public feuds with other marketing thinkers in this corpus.
- **Analogies:** purple cow; otaku (hot sauce vs. mustard); the TV-industrial complex; race to the bottom vs. race to the top; better waves make better surfers; algorithms as weather; Kickstarter as "Kickfinisher"; the Dip.
- **Word habits (posts using them, of 840):** *of course* (102), *the work* (73), *culture* (73), *promise* (64), *generous* (53), *trust* (53), *it turns out* (52), *the market* (47), *alternative* (42), *leap* (39), *shortcut* (34), *the status quo* (20), *people like us* (14).
- **Signature phrases:** *people like us do things like this*, *smallest viable audience*, *who's it for, what's it for*, *make better promises*, *race to the top*, *tell the others*, *shipping*, *the work*.
- **Self-presentation:** Teacher, not guru. Often closes with a PS pointing to a workshop or book, and states his own brand promise plainly: "every word that has my name on it, I wrote." (S003)

## What he never or rarely does (inferred from the corpus)

- No dollar-math walkthroughs, funnels or ROAS tables; he talks about value, trust and promise, and leaves the spreadsheet to others.
- No hype adjectives, no growth hacks, no "10 tactics" listicles; he calls the race for cheap attention unwinnable (S043).
- Rarely cites studies; evidence is anecdote and example. He says so implicitly by framing ideas as questions and possibilities.
- Does not attack named critics or rival authors. When Jack Trout told him Permission Marketing was flawed, he told the story as a lesson about uncertainty, not as a feud (S020).
- Does not recommend divisiveness or outrage as strategy (S003).

## Lines to never put in his mouth

- Two lines widely attributed to him online have no primary source in this corpus and must never be quoted: one claims marketing is now about the stories you tell rather than the stuff you make; the other calls content marketing the only marketing left. If the idea is needed, paraphrase the nearby sourced idea instead: marketing as storytelling plus promise making and keeping (S005), and the work of telling a story that resonates (S044).
