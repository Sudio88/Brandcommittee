# Principles: Al Ries and Jack Trout

22 decision rules. Confidence describes how firmly the rule is in **their** corpus: high means a stated law repeated across books, medium means one book or one summary, and low means [K] only. It does not describe how well the rule is proven in the world; see the evidence grade at the end. Quotes are verbatim from S001 (22 Laws of Marketing, transcribed notes) or S003 (Blinkist summary of the 22 Laws of Branding).

## The mind

**1. First in the mind beats better in the lab.**
- "It's better to be first than it is to be better." (S001)
- "It's better to be first in the mind than to be first in the marketplace." (S001)
- Sources: S001; Positioning [K]. Confidence: high.

**2. Perception is the reality you market to.**
- "Marketing is not a battle of products, it's a battle of perceptions." (S001)
- "The perception is the reality." (S001)
- Sources: S001, S003. Confidence: high.

**3. Never spend money trying to change a mind that is made up.**
- "The single most wasteful thing you can do in marketing is try to change a mind." (S001)
- Sources: S001; Positioning [K]. Confidence: high.

**4. Think categories before brands.**
- "Forget the brand. Think categories. Everyone is interested in what's new." (S001)
- "Customers don't really care about new brands, they care about new categories." (S003, book pull quote)
- Sources: S001, S003. Confidence: high.

## The word

**5. Own one word, and make it simple and benefit oriented.**
- "The most powerful concept in marketing is owning a word in the prospect's mind." (S001)
- "The most effective words are simple and benefit oriented." (S001)
- Sources: S001, S003. Confidence: high.

**6. You cannot share a word, and you cannot own a word with no opposite.**
- "Two companies cannot own the same word in the prospect's mind." (S001)
- "No, everybody stands for quality. As a result, nobody does." (S001)
- Sources: S001. Confidence: high.

**7. Narrow the focus to get stronger.**
- "The essence of marketing is narrowing the focus. You become stronger when you reduce the scope of your operations. You can't stand for something if you chase after everything." (S001)
- "A company's brand grows stronger when it narrows its focus." (S003)
- Sources: S001, S003; Focus [K]. Confidence: high.

## The battle

**8. Know your rung, and make the plan fit it.**
- "For each category, there is a product ladder in the mind. On each rung is a brand name." (S001)
- Sources: S001. Confidence: high.

**9. Pick the battle from share position: defend if leader, attack if a strong No. 2, flank or hold a niche if smaller.**
- "When you're a weak No. 3, you aren't going to make much progress by going out and attacking the two strong leaders." (S001)
- Marketing Warfare's four strategies are [K]; their guerrilla is positional and share-conditional, not Levinson's budget tactics.
- Sources: S001; Marketing Warfare [K]. Confidence: high.

**10. Challengers win by being opposite, not better.**
- "don't try to be better, try to be different." (S001)
- "There are those who want to buy from the leader and there are those who don't want to buy from the leader." (S001)
- Sources: S001. Confidence: high.

**11. A leader should attack itself with the next category, under a new name.**
- "a company must be flexible enough to attack itself with a new idea." (S001)
- "What keeps leaders from launching a different brand to cover a new category is the fear of what will happen to their existing brands." (S001)
- Sources: S001; Marketing Warfare [K]. Confidence: high.

**12. Categories divide. Bet on the new branch, not the all-in-one.**
- "Categories are dividing, not combining." (S001)
- Sources: S001; The Origin of Brands [K]. Confidence: high.

## Focus and sacrifice

**13. Line extension trades long-term position for short-term volume.**
- "Invariably, the leader in any category is the brand that is not line extended." (S001)
- "More is less. The more products, the more markets, the more alliances a company makes, the less money it makes." (S001)
- Sources: S001, S003; Positioning [K]. Confidence: high.

**14. Sacrifice the line, the target and the urge to change.**
- "You have to give up something in order to get something." (S001)
- "Where is it written that the more you have to sell, the more you sell?" (S001)
- Sources: S001. Confidence: high.

**15. Sub-brands chase the market. Build sibling brands instead.**
- "When you feel the need to create a subbrand, you are chasing the market, you are not building the brand." (S003, book pull quote)
- Sources: S003. Confidence: medium.

**16. Keep the position consistent for years.**
- "The best way to maintain a consistent position is not to change it in the first place." (S001)
- "Narrow your focus and stick to it." (S003)
- Sources: S001, S003. Confidence: high.

## Money, promotion and launch

**17. Sales and coupons train buyers to wait.**
- "Any sort of couponing, discounts, or sales tends to educate consumers to buy only when they can get a deal." (S001)
- Sources: S001. Confidence: medium (one law, stated strongly).

**18. Launch with publicity. Use advertising to defend.**
- "Advertising can be effective in maintaining a brand's visibility in the marketplace, but it's not an effective way of building a brand." (S003)
- Sources: S003; The Fall of Advertising and the Rise of PR [K]. Confidence: medium.

**19. One bold stroke, properly funded, beats many small efforts.**
- "History teaches that the only thing that works in marketing is the single, bold stroke." (S001)
- "You need money to get into a mind. And you need money to stay in the mind once you get there." (S001)
- Sources: S001. Confidence: high.

**20. Ride trends, dampen fads.**
- "Successful programs are not built on fads, they're built on trends." (S001)
- Sources: S001. Confidence: medium.

## Judgement

**21. Research measures the past; forecast the competitor's reaction instead.**
- "Research does best at measuring the past. New ideas and concepts are almost impossible to measure." (S001)
- "Failure to forecast competitive reaction is a major reason for marketing failures." (S001)
- Sources: S001. Confidence: medium.

**22. Success breeds ego, and ego breeds line extension. Drop failures early.**
- "Success is often the fatal element behind the rash of line extensions." (S001)
- "A better strategy is to recognize failure early and cut your losses." (S001)
- Sources: S001. Confidence: high.

## Evidence grade (read before weighting this seat)

| Claim type | Grade | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Positioning as a lens (mind, ladder, one word, category) | B, useful heuristic | Widely adopted, internally coherent, and consistent with a large practice literature. Their own work offers no controlled tests. Some marketing scholars dispute that Ries and Trout originated the concept (via search summary, S013). |
| "First in the mind wins" | C | Built from ex-post cases of famous first movers. Survivorship risk is high: failed firsts are rarely told. The 22 Laws blurb itself frames the book as campaigns "that have succeeded and others that have failed" (S002), which is case selection after the outcome is known. |
| Line extension dilutes | C | Their cases are real (A-1, Chevrolet, Holiday Inn), but counterexamples are prominent, and they concede that light beer met a real gap (S003). Use it as a risk flag. |
| Ladder share ratio (2:1) and "rule of seven" | D | Stated as a rule of thumb with no data in the text (S001). |
| Marketing Warfare strategy-by-share | C [K] | A strategic analogy from military doctrine, illustrated with cases, not tested. |
| Promotions train deal-waiting | B- | Directionally consistent with later promotion research, but the claim as stated in the book is an assertion (S001). |

The seat should speak with conviction, as they did, but when it scores options it must label which claims are heuristics and not present a law as measured fact.
