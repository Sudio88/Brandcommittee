# Frameworks: Al Ries and Jack Trout

18 frameworks, grouped by the job they do: get in the mind, choose the battle, protect the position, and name and launch. Book tags show where each framework sits in their books. Quotes are verbatim from S001 (near-verbatim transcription of The 22 Immutable Laws of Marketing, 1993) or S003 (Blinkist summary of The 22 Immutable Laws of Branding, 1998). [K] marks content taken from the books as widely known, paraphrased and not checked against fetched text in this build (see sources.md). [I] marks the seat's application to Moonshot.

---

## Get into the mind

### 1. Law of Leadership: first in the mind *(22 Laws, 1993; Positioning, 1981)*
- **What it is:** The first brand into the mind in a category usually stays the leader, and its name often turns generic. Being first in the mind matters more than being first on the market.
- **Parts:** First (into the mind, not only into the market). Generic-capable name. Leadership claim kept up over time.
- **When to use:** Any launch, and any time someone argues "ours is better, so we'll win."
- **How to score:** Is there a category where we are, or can credibly become, the first brand buyers name? If the plan rests on "better than the leader" with no first, score it low.
- **Quote:** "It's much easier to get into the mind first than to try to convince someone you have a better product than the one that did get there first." (S001)
- **Also:** "It's better to be first in the mind than to be first in the marketplace." (S001)
- **Sources:** S001 [F]; Positioning [K].

### 2. Law of the Category: "First what?" *(22 Laws, 1993)*
- **What it is:** If you cannot be first in a category, set up a new one you can be first in, and promote the category rather than the brand.
- **Parts:** (1) Ask "first what?" before "better how?" (2) Name the new category in words buyers already use. (3) Promote the category, because a first mover has no competitors yet.
- **When to use:** New SKU or sub-brand briefs, and any brief whose comparison table puts us against an established leader.
- **How to score:** Can a buyer say the category in two or three words? Would they search for it? Are we first in it? Three yes answers pass.
- **Quote:** "Forget the brand. Think categories. Everyone is interested in what's new." (S001)
- **Also:** "When you're the first in a new category, promote the category. In essence, you have no competition." (S001); "Customers don't really care about new brands, they care about new categories." (S003, book pull quote)
- **Sources:** S001, S003 [F].

### 3. The Ladder *(Positioning, 1981; 22 Laws, 1993)*
- **What it is:** Each category is a ladder in the mind with a brand on each rung. You make progress only by facing your real rung.
- **Parts:** Rung position. Number of rungs (low-interest, infrequent categories have few). Share ratio rule of thumb: roughly twice the brand below you and half the brand above. A "rule of seven" cap on rungs.
- **When to use:** Before any plan, and whenever a brief assumes a rung we have not earned.
- **How to score:** Write down our rung and the brands above us. A plan that ignores the rung fails.
- **Quote:** "Where are we on the ladder in the prospect's mind? On the top rung? On the second rung? Or maybe we're not on the ladder at all." (S001)
- **Also:** "You tend to have twice the market share of the brand below you and half the market share of the brand above you." (S001); "There seems to be a rule of seven in the prospect's mind." (S001)
- **Evidence note:** The 2:1 ratio is a rule of thumb, not a measured law.
- **Sources:** S001 [F].

### 4. Own one word (Law of Focus, Law of Exclusivity, Law of the Word) *(22 Laws, 1993; 22 Laws of Branding, 1998)*
- **What it is:** Burn one simple, benefit-oriented word into the mind. Two brands cannot own the same word, and a non-leader's word has to be narrow.
- **Parts:** A word that is available (no rival owns it). Simple and benefit oriented. Has a real opposite (no "quality"). One benefit invites the buyer to infer others.
- **When to use:** Brand briefs, packaging and listing titles, and KOL scripts. Use it any time a deck lists five "key messages".
- **How to score:** Ask ten buyers for one word about the brand. If the answers scatter, or point to a rival, there is no position yet.
- **Quote:** "The most powerful concept in marketing is owning a word in the prospect's mind." (S001)
- **Also:** "When a competitor owns a word or position in the prospect's mind, it is futile to attempt to own the same word." (S001); "No, everybody stands for quality. As a result, nobody does." (S001); "If you strongly establish one benefit, the prospect is likely to give you a lot of other benefits, too." (S001)
- **Sources:** S001, S003 [F].

### 5. Law of Perception *(22 Laws, 1993)*
- **What it is:** Marketing is a battle of perceptions, not products. Minds rarely change, so work with the perceptions buyers already hold.
- **Parts:** Existing perception (what buyers already believe). Fit (does our claim match it?). The trap: spending to fix a perception.
- **When to use:** When a brief says "once they try it, they'll see it's better."
- **How to score:** Does the plan require buyers to change their minds? If it does, score it low.
- **Quote:** "Marketing is not a battle of products, it's a battle of perceptions." (S001)
- **Also:** "In Japan, Honda got into customers' minds as a manufacturer of motorcycles, and apparently most people don't want to buy a car from a motorcycle company." (S001); "Quality is important but perception is the trump card." (S003)
- **Sources:** S001, S003 [F].

## Choose the battle

### 6. Duality and the Law of the Opposite *(22 Laws, 1993)*
- **What it is:** Markets settle into two-horse races. The No. 2 wins by finding the essence of the leader and offering the opposite, never by imitating.
- **Parts:** Find the leader's strength, turn it into a weakness, and present the opposite with a ring of truth. Buyers split into those who want the leader and those who don't.
- **When to use:** Attacking an established leader. Also when we are No. 3 and tempted to attack both leaders.
- **How to score:** Is our claim the opposite of the leader's essence? Will buyers grant the negative at once?
- **Quote:** "You must discover the essence of the leader and then present the prospect with the opposite." (S001)
- **Also:** "In the long run, every market becomes a two horse race." (S001); "There has to be a ring of truth about the negative if it is to be effective." (S001); "If old people drink Coke and young people drink Pepsi, there's nobody left to drink Royal Crown cola." (S001)
- **Sources:** S001 [F].

### 7. The Marketing Warfare square: defensive, offensive, flanking, guerrilla *(Marketing Warfare, 1986)* [K]
- **What it is:** Strategy follows share position. Only the market leader plays defense. A strong No. 2 (or No. 3) attacks. Smaller players flank or play guerrilla. Their best-known line on the split is that, in a typical market of 100 companies, one should defend, two attack, three flank and the rest fight as guerrillas [K, paraphrase].
- **Parts [K]:**
  - **Defensive (leader only):** the best defense is the courage to attack yourself with new products before rivals do; always block a strong competitive move; keep reserves.
  - **Offensive (No. 2 or 3):** the main issue is the strength of the leader's position, not your own; find a weakness inherent in the leader's strength and attack there; attack on as narrow a front as possible.
  - **Flanking:** move into uncontested ground (a lower or higher price point, a smaller or bigger size, a new form or channel) with tactical surprise, then pursue as hard as you attacked.
  - **Guerrilla:** find a segment small enough to defend and lead; no matter how successful you become, never act like the leader; keep an organisation lean enough to bug out at short notice.
- **Not Levinson:** this guerrilla is a share-conditional position about where to fight. It is not Jay Conrad Levinson's Guerrilla Marketing (1984), which is a low-budget tactical toolkit. Keep them separate. Budget tactics go to the Levinson seat.
- **When to use:** Any "attack the leader" or "go national/category-wide" brief, and any brief that sets a share target.
- **How to score:** Match the proposed battle to our real share and rung. A No. 5 brand planning an offensive scores low, and so does a leader that refuses to attack itself.
- **Fetched support:** "If you're shooting for second place, your strategy is determined by the leader." (S001); "In strength there is weakness. Wherever the leader is strong, there is an opportunity for a would-be No. 2 to turn the tables." (S001); "Most often there is only one place where a competitor is vulnerable." (S001)
- **Sources:** Marketing Warfare [K]; S001 [F] for the supporting laws.

### 8. Law of Division and divergence *(22 Laws, 1993; The Origin of Brands, 2004)*
- **What it is:** Categories split over time, and each new branch gets its own leader, which is rarely the old leader. The Origin of Brands extends this with Darwin: categories diverge rather than converge, so the opportunity is a new branch with a new name, not a combined "all-in-one" [K].
- **Parts:** Spot the branch (a new form, user, occasion or price tier). Get there first with its own name. The leader of the parent category covers the branch with a different brand.
- **When to use:** "Should we make a 2-in-1 or all-in-one?" briefs, and any new subcategory forming on a marketplace.
- **How to score:** Is this branch real (buyers name it and search for it)? Convergence products score low unless buyers already ask for them.
- **Quote:** "Over time, a category will divide and become two or more categories." (S001)
- **Also:** "Each segment has its own leader, which is rarely the same as the leader of the original category." (S001); "Categories are dividing, not combining." (S001)
- **Sources:** S001 [F]; The Origin of Brands [K].

### 9. Line extension test (Law of Line Extension, Law of Extensions) *(Positioning, 1981; 22 Laws, 1993; 22 Laws of Branding, 1998)*
- **What it is:** Success creates pressure to put the name on more products. In the mind, a strong brand is the product itself, so stretching it blurs what it stands for.
- **Parts:** Does the new item sit inside the word the brand owns? Does it pull the name into a different category? Who gains in the short run (the trade, the P&L) and who loses in the long run (the position)?
- **When to use:** Every new SKU, flavour, format and adjacent category.
- **How to score:** Inside the word: allowed. Outside the word: needs a new name or a no. A brand that is already the leader should be the hardest to extend.
- **Quote:** "In the mind, A-1 is not the brand name, but the steak sauce itself." (S001)
- **Also:** "Invariably, the leader in any category is the brand that is not line extended." (S001); "There's an irresistible pressure to extend the equity of the brand." (S001); "That simply muddies the waters and leaves consumers unsure what the existing brand stands for." (S003)
- **Concession in their own corpus:** "Light beer was one case where there was a clear gap in the market." (S003). Their complaint was the borrowed name, not the gap.
- **Sources:** S001, S003 [F].

### 10. Sub-brands versus a family of brands (Law of Subbrands, Law of Siblings) *(22 Laws of Branding, 1998)*
- **What it is:** A sub-brand that borrows the parent name drags both brands toward confusion. A family of separate brands, each with its own identity, works.
- **Parts:** Parent name plus a modifier (risky). A sibling brand with its own name, sold under one company (preferred). A company name kept apart from the brand names.
- **When to use:** Premium tiers, kids lines, and adjacent categories inside a brand house.
- **How to score:** Would a buyer of the premium tier find the parent name a reason to pay less? If so, give it a sibling name.
- **Quote:** "When you feel the need to create a subbrand, you are chasing the market, you are not building the brand." (S003, book pull quote)
- **Also:** "Like adult siblings pursuing their own lives, each brand should stand out in its own right and on its own merits." (S003); Holiday Inn's Crowne Plaza guests said "they thought it was a bit expensive for a Holiday Inn." (S003)
- **Sources:** S003 [F].

## Protect the position

### 11. Law of Sacrifice *(22 Laws, 1993; Focus, 1996)*
- **What it is:** You have to give something up to get something. There are three sacrifices: product line, target market, and constant change. Focus (1996) applies the same idea to whole companies [K].
- **Parts:** Cut the line. Aim at a narrow target, accepting that the market will be wider than the target. Keep the position for years instead of changing it at budget review.
- **When to use:** Range reviews, "let's appeal to everyone" briefs, and yearly rebrands.
- **How to score:** Name what the plan gives up. If it gives up nothing, it is not a position.
- **Quote:** "There are three things to sacrifice: product line, target market, and constant change." (S001)
- **Also:** "The full line is a luxury for a loser." (S001); "The target is not the market." (S001); "The best way to maintain a consistent position is not to change it in the first place." (S001)
- **Sources:** S001 [F]; Focus [K].

### 12. Law of Attributes *(22 Laws, 1993)*
- **What it is:** For every attribute there is an opposite one that can be effective. If the leader owns the key attribute, take a different one and dramatise it. Do not chase the same attribute.
- **Parts:** The most important attribute (usually taken). An available opposite or lesser attribute. Dramatising its value.
- **When to use:** When a rival already owns the obvious benefit (for example "fast-healing" or "whitening").
- **How to score:** Is our attribute ours alone, and is it worth something to buyers? Without one, compete on low price or not at all.
- **Quote:** "You must move on to a lesser attribute and live with a smaller share of the category." (S001)
- **Also:** "Without one, you had better have a low price. A very low price." (S001)
- **Sources:** S001 [F].

### 13. Law of Perspective: the cost of sales and coupons *(22 Laws, 1993)*
- **What it is:** Marketing effects play out over the long run, and promotions teach buyers to wait for a deal.
- **Parts:** Short-term lift. Long-term habit of waiting for a deal. The signal that regular prices are too high.
- **When to use:** Mega-sale calendars, voucher stacking, and "flash sale to hit the month".
- **How to score:** Does the promotion teach the buyer our real price is lower? Count the long-run cost, not only the spike.
- **Quote:** "Any sort of couponing, discounts, or sales tends to educate consumers to buy only when they can get a deal." (S001)
- **Also:** "It says that your regular prices are too high." (S001)
- **Sources:** S001 [F].

### 14. Law of Acceleration: trends over fads *(22 Laws, 1993)*
- **What it is:** Build on slow trends, not fast fads. If a business is rising like a fad, dampen it to stretch it into a trend.
- **Parts:** Is it a fad or a trend? Capacity and inventory commitments. Deliberately rationing supply.
- **When to use:** Viral SKUs, character or licence waves, and a TikTok spike.
- **How to score:** Does the plan gear up (stock, staff, fixed cost) as though a fad were a trend? Score it low.
- **Quote:** "Successful programs are not built on fads, they're built on trends." (S001)
- **Also:** "One way to maintain a long-term demand for your product is to never totally satisfy the demand." (S001)
- **Sources:** S001 [F].

### 15. Law of Candor *(22 Laws, 1993)*
- **What it is:** Admit a negative the buyer already believes, then turn quickly to a benefit.
- **Parts:** A widely perceived negative, admitted plainly, with a fast pivot to the positive.
- **When to use:** Premium price, an unfamiliar format, visible patches, and a strong taste.
- **How to score:** Is the negative one buyers already agree with? If not, the line confuses them.
- **Quote:** "When you admit a negative, the prospect will give you a positive." (S001)
- **Also:** "The purpose of candor is to set up a benefit that will convince your prospect." (S001)
- **Sources:** S001 [F].

## Name and launch

### 16. Naming: generic-capable, short, unique, not the company name *(Positioning, 1981; 22 Laws, 1993; 22 Laws of Branding, 1998)*
- **What it is:** The name is the hook that hangs the brand on the ladder. The first brand in a new category should pick a name that can work generically. Others need short, unique names, not generic descriptions, kept apart from the company name.
- **Parts:** Generic-capable for a category first. Short and unique. No "Nature's Best" style generic names. Company name kept separate from brand names (the P&G model). Positioning (1981) also warns against initials and line-extended names [K].
- **When to use:** Every new brand, sub-line and category name.
- **How to score:** Short? Unique? Says the category or the word? Not borrowed from the parent?
- **Quote:** "If you're introducing the first brand in a new category, you should always try to select a name that can work generically." (S001)
- **Also:** "To be effective, a name must be short and unique." (S003); "This states that companies which settle on a generic name are doomed to failure." (S003); "The vast majority of brand communication takes place verbally, not visually." (S003, book pull quote)
- **Sources:** S001, S003 [F]; Positioning [K].

### 17. Publicity builds, advertising defends (Law of Publicity, Law of Advertising) *(22 Laws of Branding, 1998; The Fall of Advertising and the Rise of PR, 2002)*
- **What it is:** New brands are built by publicity (third parties talking about a new category). Advertising maintains a brand once it is established. The 2002 book makes this its whole thesis [K].
- **Parts:** A newsworthy first. Third-party coverage (press then; creators and KOLs now [I]). Advertising later, to defend.
- **When to use:** Launch budgets, and any choice between paid ads and earned or creator voice.
- **How to score:** Is there a "first" that a third party would want to talk about? If not, ad money is buying a launch it cannot build.
- **Quote:** "Advertising can be effective in maintaining a brand's visibility in the marketplace, but it's not an effective way of building a brand." (S003)
- **Also:** "the purpose of advertising is to defend your gains in the marketplace once a brand has been launched." (S003); Miller Regular's $50 million launch failed for lack of anything new (S003).
- **Sources:** S003 [F]; Fall of Advertising [K].

### 18. Singularity and resources: one bold stroke, funded *(22 Laws, 1993)*
- **What it is:** In any situation only one move produces substantial results, and an idea without the money to get into the mind is worthless.
- **Parts:** Find the competitor's single point of vulnerability. Commit the whole force there. Fund it enough to get in and stay in.
- **When to use:** Budget allocation briefs that spread money across many small bets.
- **How to score:** Is there one move carrying the plan? Is it funded to win, not to try?
- **Quote:** "History teaches that the only thing that works in marketing is the single, bold stroke." (S001)
- **Also:** "Trying harder is not the secret of marketing success." (S001); "You need money to get into a mind. And you need money to stay in the mind once you get there." (S001); "Remember: An idea without money is worthless." (S001)
- **Sources:** S001 [F].
