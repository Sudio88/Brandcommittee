# Books: Al Ries and Jack Trout

Bibliographic notes. Only The 22 Immutable Laws of Marketing (via S001) and The 22 Immutable Laws of Branding (via S003) were read in this build. Every other book is described from its widely known content [K] and is never quoted. The years are first editions as commonly listed; verify them before citing in a report.

## Joint work (Ries and Trout)

### Positioning: The Battle for Your Mind (1981; 20th anniversary edition 2001)
- **Origin:** It grew out of their 1972 Advertising Age article series, "The Positioning Era" (via search summary, S006, S013). Ries credits Trout with suggesting the word "position" (via search summary, S007).
- **Core thesis [K]:** In an over-communicated society, the mind accepts only what fits what it already holds. Positioning is what you do to the mind of the prospect, not to the product. Get in first, or relate yourself to the leader's position (Avis, 7-Up). Reposition the competition. Choose names carefully, and avoid initials and line extension.
- **Frameworks here:** #1, #3, #9, #16.

### Marketing Warfare (1986)
- **Core thesis [K]:** Marketing is war against competitors, fought in the mind. Strategy is set by share position: one company defends, a few attack, some flank, and most should be guerrillas. Each strategy has its own principles (frameworks.md #7).
- **Guerrilla note:** Their guerrilla is a position, a segment small enough to lead, defined by share. It is not Jay Conrad Levinson's Guerrilla Marketing (1984), a book of low-cost tactics. The two are never merged in this brain.

### Bottom-Up Marketing (1989) and Horse Sense (1991)
- **Core thesis [K]:** Find a tactic (an angle that works in the mind) first and build the strategy around it. Horse Sense applies positioning to careers. Low relevance to this seat.

### The 22 Immutable Laws of Marketing (1993)
- **Read via S001.** The laws are Leadership, Category, Mind, Perception, Focus, Exclusivity, Ladder, Duality, Opposite, Division, Perspective, Line Extension, Sacrifice, Attributes, Candor, Singularity, Unpredictability, Success, Failure, Hype, Acceleration and Resources.
- **Bibliographic:** Published 1993. A later edition is listed in S002 as October 1994, 160 pages, ISBN 978-1861976109.
- **Frameworks here:** most of #1 to #18.

## Al Ries (with Laura Ries unless noted)

### Focus: The Future of Your Company Depends on It (Al Ries, 1996)
- **Core thesis [K]:** Companies lose focus through diversification and line extension. The way back is to narrow to one business and one idea, like the sun focused into a laser. It is the company-level version of the Law of Sacrifice (frameworks.md #11).

### The 22 Immutable Laws of Branding (1998)
- **Read via S003 (summary).** Laws include Expansion, Contraction, Publicity, Advertising, the Word, Credentials, Quality, Category, Name, Extensions, Fellowship, Generic, Company, Subbrands, Siblings, Shape, Color, Borders, Consistency, Change, Mortality and Singularity.
- **Frameworks here:** #4, #9, #10, #16, #17.

### The 11 Immutable Laws of Internet Branding (2000) [K]
- Applies the branding laws to the internet, including separate names for internet brands. Low weight.

### The Fall of Advertising and the Rise of PR (2002) [K]
- New brands are launched by publicity, and advertising maintains them. Framework #17.

### The Origin of Brands (2004) [K]
- A Darwinian view: categories diverge over time, convergence rarely wins, and the opportunity is to be first in a new branch, with a new name. Framework #8.

### War in the Boardroom (2009) [K]
- Marketing's "right-brain" thinking versus management's "left-brain" thinking. Low weight.

### Visual Hammer (Laura Ries, 2012) [K]
- A verbal nail (the word) needs a visual hammer to drive it in. Useful for packaging and thumbnails, but it is Laura Ries's book, not Al's. Low weight.

## Jack Trout (with co-authors)

### The New Positioning (with Steve Rivkin, 1995) [K]
- An update of positioning for the 1990s, with more on the mind's limits and repositioning.

### Differentiate or Die (with Steve Rivkin, 2000; 2nd ed. 2008/2010) [K]
- In a world of choice, every brand needs a real difference. Quality, customer orientation, creativity and price are not usually differentiators. Real differentiators include being first, owning an attribute, leadership, heritage, specialisation, preference and "newer generation". Library records: S026, S027.

### Big Brands, Big Trouble (2001), Trout on Strategy (2004), In Search of the Obvious (2008), Repositioning (2009) [K]
- Case collections and restatements of positioning and differentiation for leaders in trouble. Trout also wrote columns for business media; no Trout column was fetched in this build (see sources.md).
